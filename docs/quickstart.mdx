Four steps. At the end your team has a shared workspace, one skill in it, and machines that keep
themselves current — no one runs anything to receive updates.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Install the CLI">
    <Columns>
      <Column title="In a terminal">
        ```bash
        curl -fsSL https://topos.sh/install | sh
        ```
      </Column>
      <Column title="Or paste to your agent">
        ```text
        Set up Topos for us: fetch https://topos.sh/agent and follow it.
        ```
      </Column>
    </Columns>

    macOS and Linux, WSL2 on Windows; no sudo. The agent route also handles the next step for
    you — only the browser approval stays yours to click. Details and verification:
    [Install](/docs/install).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Log in — your workspace is born right there">
    ```bash
    topos login
    ```

    Your browser opens: sign in, name your workspace, and click **Create and connect** — that
    one pass is the whole setup. A workspace is your team's shared home for skills: one address
    (`https://topos.sh/<name>`), one member list, one catalog. You can also
    [self-host](/docs/self-host) the whole product — then the address is your own domain. Your
    terminal and the approval page show the same short code — a glance that they match is the
    whole security check.

    Already have a workspace, or invited by a teammate? The same command shows yours to pick
    from — one click connects — and the invitation mail's link works as the address:
    `topos login <invite-url>`. Connecting adopts everything the workspace has given you, and
    updates apply silently at the start of each agent session. There is no per-skill step to
    accept — your workspace's **Your skills** page always shows what you have and why, with an
    off switch on every row.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Share a skill">
    Point `publish` at any skill folder you already have — here, one in your agent's skills
    directory. A bare run is a preview: it prints exactly what would happen and changes nothing.

    ```bash
    topos publish ~/.claude/skills/pr-describe
    topos publish pr-describe --yes
    ```

    The workspace now owns the skill — one version history, one current version for the whole
    team, and a page at `https://topos.sh/acme/skills/pr-describe`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="It reaches your team — automatically">
    A new skill lands in **`everyone`**, the channel the whole team has, so nothing is left for
    teammates to run: their machines pick it up at the next agent session (or right away with
    `topos update`). Bring someone new in:

    ```bash
    topos invite dana@acme.com --yes
    ```

    The invitation mail carries everything they need, including a line to paste to their agent.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## From here

- **Edit freely.** Updates never overwrite your local edits. Share an improvement back with
  `topos publish pr-describe -m "Cover stacked PRs" --yes`.
- **Pin skills to a repo.** `topos init` writes the repo's [`topos.toml`](/docs/topos-toml), then
  `topos add pr-describe` records a line in it; commit the file and everyone working in that repo
  gets the same set.
- **Take one you weren't given.** **Your skills → Library** browses the whole catalog; **Add to
  mine** puts a skill in your own set on every machine you log in.
- **Check any time.** Both offline: `topos list` shows what is installed where you stand, with
  versions and sources (`-g` for your whole machine), and `topos status` is the health check —
  logins, auto-update triggers, and anything waiting on you.
